## The Reality of Owning a Home

Owning a home remains out of reach for many Nigerians, not simply because houses are expensive, but because the available routes to ownership are not always financially achievable. High property prices, borrowing costs, initial deposit requirements, and available payment arrangements can all affect whether prospective homeowners can afford to buy a property.

Understanding the barriers to homeownership also means examining the options available to prospective buyers and how well these options accommodate different financial circumstances. This article examines five routes to homeownership in Nigeria, how they work, and the barriers that may limit their reach.

## Mortgage Financing and the Cost of Borrowing

A mortgage lets a buyer purchase a home by making an initial contribution and borrowing the balance, then repaying it over an agreed period. In Nigeria, the availability of affordable financing remains a key consideration for prospective homeowners.

The Ministry of Finance Incorporated Real Estate Investment Fund (MREIF) offers eligible buyers a fixed interest rate of 9.75% per annum, repayment periods of up to 20 years, and a minimum equity contribution of 10%. By August 2026, it had disbursed ₦140 billion through 21 financial institutions, supporting 2,018 mortgages across 27 states.

The difference in access becomes clearer when we compare Nigeria with another country like the UK, where first-time buyers borrowed an average of 3.6 times their annual income in 2025, with deposits representing about 14.4% of the purchase price. Although UK housing remains very expensive, access to established mortgage financing gives eligible buyers a structured route to ownership. In Nigeria, borrowing costs, deposit requirements, and access to suitable mortgages shape who can realistically benefit from the same route.

While mortgage financing is reaching buyers in Nigeria, its requirements may still place it beyond the reach of many Nigerians who aspire to own a home.

## Off-Plan Purchases and Paying in Instalments

Off-plan purchases are one of the common pathways offered by property developers in Nigeria, allowing buyers to commit to a property before construction is completed, sometimes before work begins. Buyers typically pay an initial deposit and spread the balance over an agreed period, while developers use these payments to support construction.

For example, a developer somewhere in Lagos may offer a two-bedroom apartment for ₦60 million, requiring a ₦10 million initial deposit and spreading the remaining ₦50 million over 12 months. Payment terms vary across developers and properties, but the arrangement gives buyers time to spread the cost rather than pay the full amount upfront.

Off-plan purchases also come with risks. Inflation, rising construction costs, and unforeseen circumstances can push completion costs beyond initial estimates, leading to delays or difficulties delivering as promised. Established developers may have the financial capacity to absorb these pressures and still deliver, although this can come at a higher cost to buyers through their prices and payment terms.

Buyers are advised to verify the property's title, assess the developer's track record, and understand the terms for delays or non-completion. The model suits buyers who can raise the initial deposit and sustain payments throughout the agreed period.

## Government Supported Housing and the Question of Access

Launched in February 2024, the Federal Government's Renewed Hope Cities and Estates Programme aims to deliver 100,000 housing units nationwide through partnerships with private developers. Its official online portal allows Nigerians at home and in the diaspora to search available properties by location, type, and price, with the programme targeting low and middle-income earners, including civil servants, traders, artisans, and commercial drivers.

Eligible buyers can explore outright purchase, instalment payments, National Housing Fund (NHF) mortgages and the Federal Mortgage Bank of Nigeria's Rent to Own Scheme. The portal advertises NHF mortgages at 6% interest, alongside a non-interest Rent to Own option, subject to the relevant eligibility requirements.

The programme is progressing, although delivery remains ongoing. In June 2026, the government reported that its 2,084-unit project in Ibeju Lekki, Lagos, was between 85% and 90% complete. The Federal Ministry of Housing and Urban Development has also reported 14 active construction sites nationwide, covering 10,112 housing units. In September, 430 staff of the Nigerian Midstream and Downstream Petroleum Regulatory Authority received housing allocation letters across Abuja, Kano and Lagos. These figures indicate activity across construction, financing and access to housing, while the number of completed homes handed over remains a separate measure of the programme's delivery.

## Rent to Own and the Possibility of Turning Rent into Ownership

Rent to own remains a less familiar route to homeownership in Nigeria than conventional property purchases and developer-led instalment plans. It allows eligible occupants to live in a property while making payments towards ownership over an agreed period, reducing the need to raise the full purchase price before moving in.

The Federal Mortgage Bank of Nigeria (FMBN) offers a Rent to Own Scheme with payments spread over up to 30 years. In August 2026, 129 completed three-bedroom bungalows were handed over to beneficiaries at Nasarawa Technology Village in Karu, Nasarawa State. Of the original beneficiaries profiled for Bungalow City, 251 had remained without allocation, showing the gap between demand and available homes.

The scheme requires applicants to meet eligibility conditions, including participation in the National Housing Fund and evidence of regular income. It may be particularly useful for workers who can sustain regular payments but struggle to raise a large deposit. Its reach depends on how many suitable homes are available and whether the payment terms are manageable for the people the scheme is designed to serve.

## Lease to Own and the Commitment to Purchase

Like rent to own, lease to own allows a buyer to live in a property while working towards its purchase over an agreed period. The arrangement may involve paying annual rent alongside contributions towards the purchase price, with the remaining balance due at the end of the lease.

For example, a buyer interested in a ₦75 million property could agree to a five-year lease, paying rent while making agreed contributions towards the purchase. The contract would establish how much of these payments goes towards the purchase price and the final balance due, as the terms vary with each agreement.

The commitment runs both ways. The buyer must be able to meet the payments and settle the outstanding balance, while the property owner must honour the agreement and transfer ownership as promised. If either party fails to fulfil their obligations, disputes may arise over payments made or the transfer of the property. Clear contractual terms are therefore essential to protect both parties and give the arrangement a realistic chance of leading to ownership.

## The Fulcrum View

Expanding homeownership requires not only more homes, but also pathways that reflect how Nigerians earn, manage their finances and plan for the future. Making ownership more attainable can strengthen household financial security while supporting wider economic growth.

The rent-to-own pathway could play a greater role in bridging the gap between earning a regular income and raising the capital required to purchase a home. Nigeria already has a foundation through the Federal Mortgage Bank of Nigeria's Rent to Own Scheme, including homes handed over to beneficiaries. The opportunity is to expand this pathway through a steady supply of suitable homes and payment terms that households can realistically sustain, because spreading payments over time will not make a home affordable if its price remains beyond the buyer's means.

Realising this opportunity will require government, private developers and financial institutions to work together to align land access, housing delivery and financing with effective demand. For ordinary Nigerians, homeownership can provide more than an asset. It can offer greater stability and allow households to build towards the future while contributing to savings, investment and economic activity. The goal is a housing market in which owning a home becomes a more achievable financial milestone, supported by a model that is both commercially viable and responsive to people's needs.
