## A Changing Monetary Landscape

Over the past few years, Nigerian businesses have had to make investment and expansion decisions amid high interest rates. As borrowing costs rose, accessing finance became more expensive, making businesses more cautious about taking on credit to expand operations, increase production, or pursue new opportunities.

The Monetary Policy Rate (MPR) rose from 18.75% in July 2023 to 27.5% in November 2024, before remaining above 26% through much of 2025 and 2026. The effects of this high interest rate environment extended beyond the policy benchmark, as businesses continued to contend with the cost of accessing commercial credit.

In September 2026, the Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) reset the MPR from 26.5% to 23%, a 350 basis point adjustment intended to realign the benchmark with prevailing market rates and improve monetary policy transmission. The adjustment was somewhat significant, but early lending figures suggest that the cost of credit has not necessarily followed the same direction. The average maximum lending rate across the banking sector rose from 29.20% in August to 32.62% in September.

This raises a more important question about what a lower policy rate actually means for businesses seeking finance, and whether the adjustment can translate into more affordable credit for productive investment.

## The Implications for Real Estate Development

For property developers, financing is central to the entire development process. Before construction begins, capital is required to acquire land, verify titles, secure planning approvals and prepare the site. As development progresses, further investment goes into infrastructure, materials, labour and other construction expenses. These costs accumulate over time, while the returns on the investment may only begin to come in when completed units are sold or leased.

The financing challenge also extends to prospective homeowners. For buyers who depend on mortgage financing, high interest rates and the cost of accessing credit can make homeownership more difficult, limiting the number of people able to purchase completed properties. This creates another consideration for developers, as the viability of a project depends not only on their ability to finance and complete construction, but also on the purchasing power of the market they are building for.

This becomes particularly important when the duration of a development exceeds the repayment period of the financing available. Some Nigerian developers have reported financing costs running into billions of naira on projects that take several years to complete, while others have withdrawn from bank financing arrangements when interest rates made projects less viable. At the same time, prospective buyers facing expensive credit may take longer to commit to purchases, affecting how quickly developers recover their investments.

The September adjustment could gradually improve financing conditions on both sides of the market, but its real value will depend on whether businesses can access credit suited to the timelines of property development and prospective homeowners can secure financing that makes property acquisition more affordable.

## The Fulcrum View

A more supportive financing environment will be important to the next phase of growth in Nigeria's real estate market. As inflation moderates and economic stability improves, the longer-term ambition should be to see the MPR move towards the 15% range, a level Nigeria has operated within before. Sustaining this downward movement could create more favourable conditions for businesses to access capital and commit to long-term investments.

For established real estate developers, this presents an opportunity to prepare for the growth that improved financing conditions could encourage. Maintaining a pipeline of viable projects, understanding where demand is emerging and strengthening the capacity to deliver will be important as market conditions change.

The opportunity is not simply to wait for borrowing costs to fall, but to build the capacity to respond when the investment environment becomes more favourable. Developers who combine sound and strategic project selection with a clear understanding of market demand could be better positioned to take advantage of the next growth cycle.
